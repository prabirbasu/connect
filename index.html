When something feels different

01

A broader clinical view

The symptom may be sexual. The assessment should be broader.

Erectile function and sexual wellbeing can intersect with vascular health, diabetes, blood pressure, metabolic health, hormones, medications, sleep, psychological factors and relationships.

That does not mean every sexual symptom indicates serious disease. It means good care starts with a careful history, appropriate examination and targeted investigation — not assumptions.

Frequently asked questions

Before you apply

Is the consultation confidential?

Yes. Sexual and reproductive health concerns are handled as confidential medical information.

Do erection problems always mean low testosterone?

No. Erectile dysfunction can have multiple contributing factors. Hormonal assessment is useful when clinically indicated, but testosterone should not be assumed to be the cause without appropriate evaluation.

Will I automatically need multiple tests?

No. Investigations should be targeted to your history, examination, risk factors and clinical findings rather than ordered routinely without indication.

Is this only for erectile dysfunction?

No. Andrology includes a broader range of male sexual and reproductive concerns including libido, hormonal issues, male infertility and other specialist men's health problems.

Does applying guarantee a particular treatment?

No. Treatment recommendations depend on clinical assessment. No specific medication, procedure or outcome can be guaranteed in advance.
